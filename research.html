Research

I study classical computational problems through a quantum lens: primarily problems in P, and eventually selected NP-type problems. The goal is to pin down their optimal quantum complexity from both sides, by designing the best possible quantum algorithms and by proving quantum lower bounds and fine-grained limitations.

Classical fine-grained complexity is built on tight equivalences. All-Pairs Shortest Paths, for example, is subcubically equivalent to the Negative-Weight Triangle problem. The reductions behind these equivalences often break in the quantum setting.

Current Directions

  1. Quantum fine-grained complexity of APSP

    I study quantum analogues of the APSP and Negative-Weight Triangle (NWT) conjectures and how they relate to each other. I also study variants of APSP suited to quantum algorithms: for example, constructing a unitary that computes the distance of any queried pair on demand, or computing the distance of a uniformly random pair. Tightness in one direction between the quantum APSP and quantum NWT conjectures can be derived from existing results; I am investigating whether the other direction is tight as well. Beyond APSP, the same questions arise for other fine-grained conjectures such as 3-SUM.

  2. Structured search: quantum walks and divide and conquer

    Grover’s algorithm is optimal for unstructured search, but many problems have structure: geometry, constraints, or recursive organization. Quantum walks and quantum divide-and-conquer frameworks are ways to exploit it. For a concrete classical problem, the task is to find the right state space, walk operator and recursive combiner that make these tools apply. Directions I am interested in include relaxed Grover-like diffusion conditions (which problem-derived operators satisfy usable spectral promises, and what it costs when diffusion is not exact) and extending quantum divide and conquer to combining functions beyond AND/OR and SWITCH-CASE, including when super-quadratic speedups are possible.

  3. Matching algorithms with lower bounds

    I pair quantum algorithms with quantum query lower bounds to establish fine-grained quantum limits for problems in P.

Write-ups & Working Notes

  • PhD comprehensive exam · Working draft · Sep 2026

    Analyzing Complexity Class P through a Quantum Lens: Fine-Grained Bounds and APSP

    A self-contained account of the subcubic equivalence between All-Pairs Shortest Paths and Negative-Weight Triangle detection (Vassilevska Williams and Williams), proved through the chain APSP → MPMM → APNWT → NWT-Find → NWT-Det. For every reduction it records the number of oracle calls, adaptivity, oracle-instance parameters and overhead, and tracks how the size and weight parameters (n, W) evolve. The quantum analysis is in progress.

  • Notes · Working draft

    Classical and Quantum Random Walks

    Notes that build from classical random walks (Pólya’s recurrence theorem, spectral gaps, mixing, conductance and Cheeger’s inequality, effective resistance, search for marked vertices) to discrete- and continuous-time quantum walks on general graphs. Worked examples include the glued-trees problem and spatial search on the hypercube and the grid.

  • Instance compression · Two write-ups

    Label Cover to CVP Reduction

    A reduction from Label Cover to the Closest Vector Problem through an intermediate constrained integer minimization problem, with reductions between two Label Cover variants (Arora–Babai–Stern–Sweedyk; Khot) and between the minimization and maximization versions, a summary of Label Cover hardness results, and notes on parallel repetition.

    • PDF

    A Proposed Conjecture on Projection Games

    A conjecture intermediate between the Moshkovitz–Raz theorem and the Projection Games Conjecture: SAT on inputs of size n reduces to projection games of size n1+o(1)·poly(1/ε) over an alphabet of size 2polylog(1/ε), with soundness error ε. Through the projection-games-to-CVP reduction, this gives CVP instances of rank nO(1)·2polylog(n), in agreement with the best rank bounds known without assuming the Projection Games Conjecture.

Talks

  1. From Grover Search to Quantum Walks: Search under Structure

    Department of Computer Science and Engineering Symposium, IIT Delhi · 18–19 May 2026

    Grover’s algorithm gives an optimal quadratic speedup for unstructured search. This talk asks what changes when the search space has structure, and presents quantum walks as an algorithm-design framework rather than only a tool for searching large input graphs. The examples are the glued-trees graph of Childs, Farhi and Gutmann and Ambainis’ quantum-walk algorithm for element distinctness. The talk closes with quantum divide and conquer and open directions.
